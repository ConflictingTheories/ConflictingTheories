# Language Ontologies ⭐

~ 20250408T121207 ~

One of the hobbies which I have been looking into recently, is that of teaching myself Ancient Greek and Latin. I have always been fascinated with their cultures, history, stories, and pantheon of mythology and legendary religious deities. Learning their language with the purpose of reading some of the works and ideas in their original form, is something which I am very eager to try my hand at.

It had led me to get a number of books, and begin a sort of 'immersive' style of comprehension, whereby you read and slowly form the ideas naturally, as opposed to focusing on grammar or lexical theory.

So far, diving into these languages has really exploded a number of areas which I had loosely thought about, but never really concretely identified their importance.

Ontological networks - the relationship between ideas and meaning and other ideas is really a broad yet poorly discussed topic.

Its fascinating to me that one singular field of study (ontological research) can effectively bridge almost all aspects of our modern day experiences.

--

What is a language? What is a meme? What is an emotion? What is an idea?

What relationships exist? Why does something make us feel the way it does? How does reframing change the perspective?

How does the computer as an idea and concept related outside of technological manufacturing and employment?

How does political bias form? What justifies war? Is something 'true' and what does 'true' mean in the context? Does it imply thematic values? Does it depend on implicit axioms?

What is extreme? What is normal? How does one belief system turn into another?

--

The knowledge graph of our collective understandings are disjointed and contradictory, yet so too is our nature. Deep research into ontological frameworks is the key to the future of our species. ML/AI/LLMs are dependent on proper ontologies, and so too, frankly, are we!
